Conditions travel with the method

  • Card sampling: fixed finite-variance probability model, IID draws with replacement. Changing n/model/rule clears incompatible results; R is repetition count.
  • CLT and intervals: original non-Normal population stays unchanged. Small-n Normal-approximation coverage can be poor. Known-sigma Normal mean inference is exact under its model; estimated-s here is a stated large-sample approximation, not a t procedure.
  • Two means / Normal combination: independence is required for variance addition without covariance. Coefficients are squared in variance, even when negative.
  • Correlation tables: Pearson inference assumes independent pairs from a bivariate Normal population. Spearman no-ties table inference is unavailable with ties. A manipulated synthetic scatterplot is an illustration, not evidence of random population sampling. Table entries are one-tailed; two-tailed total alpha uses alpha/2. A threshold is not an exact p-value.
  • Discrete tests: inclusive integer tails. Two-sided p doubles the smaller inclusive tail (capped1); critical regions allocate alpha/2 to each side without moving unused probability. Actual null size can be smaller than nominal alpha.
  • Chi-square: asymptotic reference; every expected count must meet the stated frequency condition. Merge only meaningful whole categories in O and E. Raw-four-class fitted Binomialp followed by actual pooling has unsupported standard calibration; descriptive statistic remains but p/decision/referenceDF are withheld. Fixed-margin contingency Monte Carlo is a different conditional experiment, not a GOF workaround.
  • Finite frame: no replacement within a sample. Population weights are not rounded sample proportions; missing strata do not become zero. Systematic random start can remain design-unbiased under periodic ordering while variance changes. One convenience selection's error does not prove a universal quota-bias theorem.
