# Screenshot provenance

Developer PNGs in `evidence/` are development evidence, not independent reviews, real students or physical-device trials. Historical failure images intentionally remain in the full source archive. A filename alone is not proof that a PNG represents the latest application.

The freeze's selected presentation images include `final-sampling-desktop.png`, `final-sampling-mobile.png`, `final-sampling-teacher.png`, `final-guide-desktop.png`, `final-guide-mobile.png`, `freeze-gof-withheld-teacher.png`, `freeze-stratified-mobile-scrolled.png` and `final-design-compare-teacher.png`. The associated actual-browser scripts/results, source changes and capture history remain in `tests/`, `evidence/` and DEVELOPMENT-LOG.md.

The packaging helper also retains selected `stage5-design-390.png` and `stage5-normal-1920.png` images. Those are explicitly earlier development baselines; they should not be used to approve later sticky-table, type-size or final-format changes. Retaining them does not transfer a later independent approval to those pixels.

Independent images are separate: the phase5 actual-browser archive is `libfile_769f44ef833c8191a359f6cc4222672d`, fixed94d0b8d with59 original PNGs. The visual reviewer directly inspected15 of those59 and scored that baseline86.8/100. Earlier phase2/3/4 archives have their own fixed commits and reported coverage. The final accc8f9 browser audit is now preserved in FINAL-BROWSER-ACCEPTANCE-accc8f9.md. Its61 original PNGs and three selected views remain only in the isolated review environment: the official Library batch and one authorized retry failed at read-only discovery before prepare/finalize, so no final independent image IDs exist. This is separate from the successful actual browser acceptance. Do not relabel an earlier archive or developer images as that unuploaded independent collection.

For a classroom handoff, prioritize the final Sampling desktop/phone views and the exact-version independent final screenshots. No screenshot establishes measured student understanding, a real projector's back-row readability or screen-reader speech.
